# Schedule 1 — Drafting Justifications

**For Homeowner review only — not for inclusion in the contractual agreement.** This document must not be attached to, incorporated into, or included in the Agreement or its signing package.

These notes explain [Schedule 1](schedule_1.md). They do not add contractual obligations or independently establish the proposed assurances as true. References use the revised clause numbers: the former empty clause 3.6 has been removed and the Hanyong provisions renumbered.

## 1. Immediate and post-verification warranties — clauses 1.1–1.4, 1.11 and 2.5

The Agreement binds the parties at signing so Roque must investigate, disclose and deliver the package within the agreed 14-day period. The signing warranties in clause 1.11 and P.E. warranties in clause 2.5 arise immediately at signing. All other factual warranties arise only after the required verification and review, when the complete package and corporate adoption have reached both Homeowners; those later warranties are not backdated to the Effective Date. Historical warranties still concern the original events and periods, even though given later.

This lets Roque correct an unsupported proposed proposition before warranting it. A correction before a post-verification warranty commences is not itself a breach of that proposed warranty. This does not defer or excuse breach of an immediate P.E. warranty. It can nevertheless give the Homeowners grounds to decline the proposed arrangement or terminate under section 5. The main agreement's separate admission concerning the Original Contract remains unchanged.

Completing enquiries is not the same as resolving every factual issue. Except for the immediate P.E. requirements, a package may satisfy delivery while accurately identifying genuine uncertainty after the required enquiries. Roque warrants the accuracy of its enquiries, evidence and account of that uncertainty, rather than warranting an unresolved proposition as true. The Homeowners must expressly accept a corrected or unresolved basis before Roque proceeds. An incomplete investigation cannot be relabelled an unresolved finding to avoid the verification obligations.

## 2. Proportionate evidence and director review — clauses 1.5–1.8

The purpose is a reviewable account with reliable sources, not multiple reports repeating the same material. A consolidated report, indexed records and email confirmations of new material accounts are sufficient. Historical document authors need not each provide a fresh signed report. Newly reported oral accounts that cannot be confirmed must be identified as such and cannot alone resolve a material disputed fact.

The director must still assess the sources, inconsistencies and limits of each conclusion. Lack of recollection or a PM title cannot prove that conduct did not occur. The current signature is a corporate adoption for Roque; it does not create a separate personal warranty or guarantee. Whether an additional personal factual confirmation is needed remains expressly on the [todo list](schedule_1-todo.md). That decision must be made before execution rather than inferred from the word “personal”.

## 3. Existing P.E. endorsements — section 2

The P.E. requirements are immediate warranties rather than matters left for the 14-day investigation. For each document, Roque must already have and supply both Homeowners with authentic written P.E. confirmation of authorised use and present suitability at signing. An attributable P.E. email or message is sufficient; a verbal assurance relayed by Hanyong or Roque is insufficient. This does not assume Hanyong's earlier statement was false: verbal confirmation and the contractual requirement for written evidence are distinct.

Absence or non-delivery of the required written confirmation, or a P.E. denial or withdrawal of authorisation or suitability, is an immediate breach triggering automatic termination under clause 5.13. If present at signing, the Agreement first becomes binding and then terminates; accrued obligations are preserved. Either Homeowner or Roque may notify the other side, and Roque must promptly do so on learning of the trigger. Notification records termination rather than postponing it. No cure period or corrected/unresolved disclosure bypasses the requirement. A later confirmation cannot revive the Agreement without a fresh signed arrangement.

The distinction between an affirmative confirmation with disclosed implementation requirements and refusal to confirm present suitability prevents a request for routine implementation steps from being mistaken for rejection. It does not allow a response that reserves suitability pending further assessment to count as confirmation.

The Homeowners report that Roque proposes to reuse endorsements already completed approximately 12 months ago; the available notes identify September 2025 documents. Exact dates, revisions and coverage still need to be confirmed. The proposal concerns use of existing documents, not an assumption that new endorsements must be commissioned.

There is no separate need to know whether Roque commissioned both documents: that fact does not affect whether the Homeowners would agree to this arrangement. The commissioning warranty and standalone enquiry have therefore been removed. Records concerning commissioning need only be considered if they are necessary to establish actual rights or authorisation to use the documents.

What matters is authorised use and present suitability for the installed works. The report must identify the works and revisions covered, the P.E.'s evidence and its limitations, differences from the installation, further requirements and the relevance of reported railing corrosion or other changes.

The fact that an endorsement already exists does **not**, by itself, establish that a new inspection is unnecessary. Reuse without a site visit may be entirely workable if the P.E.'s assessment and applicable HDB requirements support it. The agreement therefore records no new visit as the proposed basis, subject to verification, rather than guaranteeing that no visit can be required. Additional homeowner assistance or fees require prior written agreement. HDB treats the permit application and approval process as a separate requirement; an existing P.E. document does not itself establish approval. See [HDB's application guidance](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/managing-my-home/renovation-and-maintenance/renovation/application-for-a-renovation-permit) and [renovation information](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/managing-my-home/renovation-and-maintenance/renovation/important-information-on-renovations). These general sources do not decide the inspection requirements for these particular works.

## 4. Instructions, performers, notice and chronology — clauses 3.1–3.5 and 3.9

An account that Roque failed to instruct permit applications must not be confused with an account that it did not instruct the physical works. The verification now addresses each work item separately: who instructed and performed it, who was responsible for permits, what Saege actually said about instructions, and the exact HDB notice, its stated basis and response. Accounts relayed through others must be identified as such. Differences must be reconciled with evidence or left expressly unresolved.

The HDB notice may concern Saege's contractor or supervisory role and does not necessarily establish physical performance. The draft therefore requires examination of its wording rather than assuming a contradiction simply because Saege denies physically performing the works.

Saege's stated original involvement is the Homeowners' reason for considering it specifically. If it neither performed the works nor instructed its subcontractors to do so, that stated basis for preferring it falls away. Prior involvement is not, however, proof of current suitability or eligibility. Clause 3.9 retains the proposed performance assurance, subject to verification and correction.

Regina's roles, actual functions and company relationships must be separately verified. Her title alone does not explain why permits were missing or why the Homeowners were not told. The chronology must connect instructions, permit checks and applications, installation, P.E. documents, handover, discovery and notification, separating planned from actual events. The June and July calendar entries and September endorsements require explanation but do not independently prove when the acts occurred. The August glass-installation entry must not be assumed to concern the awning.

The former clause 3.6 has been deleted because it did not identify any additional investigation issue.

## 5. Hanyong, Nicholas, Cayden and the specified personnel — clauses 3.6–3.8

The proposed assurance is now confined to management or coordination functions for the staircase railings and balcony awning, separately for Roque and Saege. It no longer covers unrelated coordination across the whole renovation. Actual instructions, sequencing, contractor coordination, P.E./permit arrangements, approval checks and commencement decisions matter more than a title. Passing on an isolated message is not automatically project management, although a message may itself contain a relevant instruction or authorisation.

Clause 3.6 states a direct proposed factual assurance. The verified account expressly adopted by Roque is the contractual assurance, subject to section 5; comparison with particular WhatsApp messages is not a standalone prerequisite. Differences in wording or scope need not matter, but a known material factual inconsistency with an earlier account must still be disclosed and explained under clause 3.7 and section 5. Relevant messages remain evidence where they bear on that issue. Adoption does not release claims concerning an earlier statement or retrospectively qualify a warranty already given.

The Homeowners also rely on Nicholas and Cayden. They are identified in the available records as directors of Roque and Saege respectively, but their full identities, relevant appointment periods and actual capacities must be verified. Clause 3.7 now requires a separate account for each of them alongside Hanyong, covering actual functions, permit responsibilities, delegation and follow-up, authorisations, checks, knowledge, disclosure, representations and charging. It also requires identification and verification of any material assurances they themselves made. The proposed assurance concerning Hanyong's absence of management or coordination involvement is not assumed to apply to either director.

Clause 3.8 requires their intended verification, oversight and regularisation responsibilities and decision-making authority to be specified. This makes the basis of homeowner reliance explicit even if neither will be a PM. The suitability, consent and termination provisions therefore extend to specified directors, representatives and verification or oversight leads, expressly including Nicholas and Cayden. Roque gives the verified factual warranties about their conduct; naming them does not create a separate personal warranty or guarantee. The existing todo concerning whether an additional personal factual confirmation is required remains open.

The earlier replacement requirement depended on a combination of PM status, omitted permit instructions and authorisation to proceed. That narrow combined trigger has been replaced by disclosure, consent and termination provisions addressing material suitability findings, regardless of title. Identify all specified regularisation managers, directors, representatives and verification or oversight leads and their authority before signing. The Homeowners may consider replacements but need not accept one instead of exercising a valid termination right.

## 6. Roque and Saege's conduct, including non-PMs — section 4

The Homeowners need to know who instructed, authorised or agreed that the unpermitted Specified Works proceed because that person's identity and involvement affect whether they would engage or continue with Roque, accept Saege as subcontractor and accept the specified personnel. Clause 4.1 deliberately retains the stronger proposed assurance that neither company authorised the works while the required permit was absent, regardless of checks, belief or knowledge. The concern is not confined to authorisation without checking or intentional disregard of permit obligations, and does not depend on a PM title.

The investigation and proposed assurances therefore address Roque and Saege and each Specified Work separately, expressly including non-PM directors, employees and representatives. The verified account separates authorisation, actual checks and knowledge, with dates, company capacities, recipients, scope and supporting records. An assumption that someone else handled permits must not be recast as an actual check. A genuine check or honest mistake may affect the Homeowners' assessment of the conduct, but cannot make the stronger negative assurance true if the required permit was absent. Identifying authorisation does not itself establish dishonesty or intentional disregard. Roque must correct the proposed assurance where the evidence requires it; a correction before adoption is not itself a warranty breach, and proceeding on that corrected basis requires both Homeowners' express written acceptance under clause 5.2. Any termination remains governed by section 5's stated requirements.

Authorising without checking and knowingly proceeding without permits are distinct from an administrative omission. Concealment, inaccurate statements and charging for services already intended not to be performed are also separate questions. Saege's relevant charges may involve Roque rather than the Homeowners; identify the actual payer and payee instead of assuming direct homeowner charges. Absence of charges can be recorded if verified.

These remain proposed factual assurances, not findings of wrongdoing. Roque gives the adopted warranties about both companies. Saege is not made a contracting party merely by being described in those warranties. The facts and any disputed legal characterisation must be distinguished; disclosure and the contractual termination decision do not depend on obtaining a prior statutory conviction or regulatory finding.

## 7. Current eligibility and ongoing changes — clauses 1.9 and 3.10

The Homeowners need a present basis for accepting the proposed contractor, applicant, P.E. and managers. Verify Saege's relevant registration, restrictions and outstanding directions concerning these works and identify who will submit the application and their authority. Check current evidence and recheck relevant status before submission. Historical involvement alone is insufficient.

From signing until completion or termination, Roque must promptly disclose material discoveries or changes, expressly including the intervals between signature, adoption, delivery and submission, and discoveries after submission. A correction must reach the recipient of an affected submission, with both Homeowners copied. Materially changed arrangements require express homeowner acceptance before further action. Updated warranties arise only after verification and delivery of a signed corporate supplement; disclosure alone cannot erase an earlier false warranty.

## 8. Acceptance and unilateral termination — section 5

An unresolved issue may later produce facts that reasonably justify rejecting the arrangement, including Roque, Saege, the specified managers, directors, representatives or verification or oversight leads (expressly including Nicholas and Cayden), or the proposed method. Requiring homeowner acceptance of a disclosure would prevent proceeding, but would not by itself clearly end the binding agreement. The express termination right addresses that gap.

Either Homeowner may give written notice to Roque, copied to the other Homeowner, identifying the resolved or sufficiently clarified matter, available evidence and reasonable grounds for not continuing. Roque's agreement and a prior court determination are not prerequisites. Mere suspicion or continued uncertainty alone does not satisfy this particular trigger; while uncertainty remains, the consent restriction still applies. Accepting an unresolved risk is not advance acceptance of all adverse facts that might later emerge.

The clause 5.4 right is available even if Roque honestly reports an adverse result before the relevant post-verification warranty arises. Immediate P.E. breaches instead trigger clause 5.13. It is separate from a false-assurance dispute and from Schedule 2's evidential requirements for fee repayment. The Homeowners are not compelled to accept replacement contractors, managers or other specified personnel. Termination ends this regularisation arrangement, preserves original-contract claims and accrued rights, stops further use of consents, and requires handover of submission records. It does not automatically cancel HDB approvals or override legally required or immediate safety action.

The notice mechanism expressly allows **either** Homeowner to exercise the right. This is distinct from the requirement for **both** Homeowners to accept a corrected basis or replacement before regularisation proceeds.

Clause 5.13 preserves accrued rights, claims, payment liabilities (including sums not yet due), warranties already given, the Original Contract admission and reservation, records handover, evidence preservation and fee accounting. The cessation duties and Roque's challenge procedure apply to P.E. termination as specified there. A disputed trigger does not suspend interim duties. Where a trigger is discovered later, the accounting clock runs from notification so that an undiscovered past event does not retrospectively make the accounting overdue. Termination itself does not conclusively establish disputed falsity for Schedule 2.

## 9. Fee treatment and remaining drafting decisions — section 6

Timing is principally dealt with in section 1 and the main agreement; fee treatment is principally dealt with in Schedule 2. Schedule 1 now uses a short cross-reference instead of repeating the fee mechanics. Work earns fees, and unperformed work remains refundable. Termination does not automatically establish a false assurance or permit retention of unearned advances.

Complete the [todo list](schedule_1-todo.md), particularly the personal-confirmation decision, exact evidence and identity details, manager specifications and notice email address. The existing [drafting notes](drafting_notes.md) retain the need to settle the dispute process and obtain review of the termination and fee provisions. These notes explain the intended drafting and do not assert guaranteed legal enforceability.

## 10. Other immediate warranties — clauses 1.11 and 5.14

Identity and authority, current eligibility, Saege's written participation agreement, agreed personnel arrangements, document authenticity and disclosure of known obstacles concern the present basis for signing. Roque must establish those matters at signing rather than defer them to the historical investigation. Limited consultation of its relevant representatives and review of existing records define the signing enquiry and prevent a narrow claim that the signing director personally knew nothing. Roque warrants that these enquiries occurred. Historical responsibility, authorisations, knowledge and misconduct remain subject to full verification; known contrary information must nevertheless be disclosed immediately.

Document authenticity does not guarantee every third-party assertion. Current personnel arrangements do not establish historical functions. Saege's agreement to participate does not make it a party. Disclosed and expressly agreed exceptions to specified non-P.E. warranties are narrowly recorded at signing; general knowledge or silence is insufficient. None of these exceptions overrides the immediate P.E. requirements.

Material breach of clause 1.11 gives either Homeowner a termination right by notice under clause 5.14, without waiting for verification or resolution of a previously unresolved issue. It does not automatically end the agreement for a minor documentary error. Existing challenge procedures, interim duties, accrued rights and liabilities, payment accounting and Original Contract reservations apply. Payment, disclosure, verification and restrictions on premature action remain immediate contractual duties rather than being relabelled warranties.
