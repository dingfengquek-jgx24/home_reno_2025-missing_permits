# Schedule 1 — Drafting Justifications

**For Homeowner review only — not for inclusion in the contractual agreement.** This document must not be attached to, incorporated into, or included in the Agreement or its signing package.

These notes explain the drafting purpose of [Schedule 1 — Material Assurances Concerning Roque and Saege](schedule_1.md). They are not part of the Agreement, do not add contractual obligations, and do not independently establish that any proposed assurance is true. Clause references refer to the current Schedule 1 draft.

## 1. Why the assurances are material — clauses 1.1–1.7

The Homeowners' decision is whether to continue engaging Roque and accept Saege's involvement after the original permit failures. That decision depends on the companies' conduct, reliability and suitability, as well as whether the proposed regularisation can succeed. A successful application would not, by itself, answer concerns about an inaccurate account of the original works or a person's involvement in those failures.

The draft therefore identifies the assurances as material to that decision, warrants their factual accuracy and addresses material omissions. It also distinguishes a material inaccuracy from a minor error unrelated to the Homeowners' willingness to proceed. The attribution provision in clause 1.7 includes persons acting for Roque without assuming that an unresolved individual role belonged to Roque rather than Saege.

## 2. Verification, evidence and timing — clauses 1.4–1.5 and 1.8–1.11

The Homeowners need a reviewable written account before regularisation proceeds. An unsupported oral assurance or a promise to check later would not provide that basis. The verification package therefore identifies the people involved, their relevant knowledge, their individually confirmed accounts, the records relied upon and any limitations or unresolved matters.

The signing director must oversee and assess the checks. This addresses the risk of a conclusion being repeated by someone who was not involved, or of lack of knowledge being treated as proof that something did not occur. The director's review supplements Roque's factual warranties; merely performing an enquiry does not make an inaccurate underlying assurance acceptable.

The draft separates the Effective Date, when the underlying factual warranties apply, from the Verification Date, when completed checks are confirmed and factual warranties repeated. The Agreement takes effect on signing, while delivery of the complete package is required before regularisation proceeds. This preserves an obligation to deliver the evidence rather than making the Agreement depend on Roque first supplying it.

## 3. P.E. documents and their proposed use — clauses 2.1–2.4

The proposed process depends on identified P.E. documents. The assurances address who commissioned them, Roque's authority to use them, and confirmation from the preparing or endorsing P.E. of their suitability for the specified installed works and proposed regularisation. Exact document references and disclosed limitations are necessary to make those statements verifiable.

These provisions concern the documents and their use. They do not promise a particular HDB decision or permanent immunity from later regulatory action.

## 4. Original permit failures, project roles and performance of the works — clauses 3.1–3.6 and 3.10

Roque's account that it failed to instruct Saege to obtain the permits needs investigation and an express warranty of accuracy. That account must not obscure any separate or shared responsibility of Saege or another person. The draft accordingly requires a specific account of instructions, knowledge and involvement.

Identifying Regina as project manager does not settle whom she represented, explain why the Homeowners were not informed earlier, or exclude other people from performing management duties. Clauses 3.3–3.5 require those matters to be addressed separately. Clause 3.6 remains a placeholder: its further investigation issue must be identified before signing or its deletion expressly agreed.

Clause 3.10 expressly identifies Saege as having performed the original replacement of the staircase railings and balcony awning, directly or through its own subcontractors acting on its instructions. It applies separately to each work item and permits a combination of direct and subcontracted work. Requiring the actual performers, scopes and instructions to be identified makes the assurance verifiable.

The Homeowners' reason for considering Saege specifically for regularisation is its stated involvement in carrying out the original works. If Saege neither performed those works directly nor instructed its subcontractors to perform them, that basis for preferring Saege falls away. On the rationale presently advanced, there would be no special reason to select Saege over another contractor, while the risks and concerns associated with engaging it would remain.

In particular, the Homeowners report that HDB sent Saege a notice concerning failure to obtain the required HDB permit. An account that Saege had no involvement in performing the works would create an apparent inconsistency with that notice and would require a specific explanation. The notice's exact wording and basis should be checked against the account of who performed and instructed the works; the notice alone is not treated here as proof that Saege physically performed them. An unexplained contradiction would affect the Homeowners' confidence in the accuracy of the account and their willingness to accept Saege for regularisation.

Clause 3.10 therefore supports the Homeowners' decision whether there is a sufficient factual reason to select Saege despite those concerns. It is a proposed factual warranty for Roque to adopt or specifically correct, rather than an independent finding by the Homeowners; it does not, by itself, determine responsibility for obtaining the permits.

## 5. Hanyong's original role and the decision to continue with Roque — clauses 3.7–3.9

### Source and scope of the assurance

The Homeowners report that Hanyong previously stated over WhatsApp that he was not assigned as PM and did not perform as PM for either Roque or Saege during the original renovation works. Clause 3.7 records the substance of that reported assurance for Roque to adopt and warrant. The exact messages have not been identified or reproduced in these notes; the draft requires their dates, participants and copies in the verification package, together with Hanyong's full name.

Both parts of the assurance matter. A person may lack a formal PM appointment yet perform project-management duties in practice. The assurance therefore addresses appointment or assignment and actual functions separately, regardless of title, and applies to each company throughout the original works. Regina's identification as PM is insufficient on its own to establish either proposition about Hanyong.

### Why his actual involvement must be investigated

The Homeowners' concern extends to who was responsible for permit instructions and decisions to proceed with the works. Clause 3.8 requires an account of Hanyong's actual role, authority, instructions, omissions and knowledge. A finding that he held no PM title must not substitute for investigating those matters.

This wording does not assert that Hanyong instructed Saege to proceed without permits. It requires that possibility, and his involvement more generally, to be checked against relevant accounts and contemporaneous records, with discrepancies or uncertainty disclosed expressly.

### Why this affects the regularisation manager

The Homeowners have explained that they rely on Hanyong's assurance when deciding to continue with Roque while he represents Roque and manages regularisation. Their stated example is that, if he was also PM during the original works, failed to instruct Saege to obtain the required permits, and told Saege to proceed without them, they would not want to continue with him managing regularisation.

Clause 3.9 makes that reliance explicit. If that combination of circumstances is established, Roque must nominate a different manager, identify the person's role and authority, and obtain both Homeowners' written acceptance before proceeding. The purpose is to let the Homeowners assess the proposed replacement rather than require them to accept whoever Roque names.

The replacement requirement in the current wording has a combined trigger; it is not an automatic replacement requirement for every possible inaccuracy in clause 3.7. Other exceptions still require the section 5 disclosure and acceptance process. Appointing a replacement does not itself correct a false historical assurance or release an accrued claim.

## 6. Permit-related decisions, disclosure and charging — clauses 4.1–4.5

An omission to instruct Saege does not, by itself, establish a deliberate decision to proceed without permits, concealment, a false statement about approval, or charging for services already intended not to be performed. The draft treats those as separate factual assurances so that an explanation of the administrative failure does not leave the more serious concerns unanswered.

These assurances concern the original conduct. Their materiality does not depend on regularisation ultimately failing.

## 7. Specific exceptions and corrections — section 5

The assurances must be capable of correction where the evidence does not support them. Each exception must identify the relevant clause and actual facts, rather than rely on a general reference to previous discussions. Both Homeowners must expressly accept a proposed later exception before Roque proceeds on that basis.

This process is intended to prevent a verification report from unilaterally rewriting an assurance. Accepting a disclosed fact or reviewing a report does not itself release claims, and later disclosure does not retrospectively qualify an earlier warranty.

## 8. Relationship with the assistance fee — section 6

Schedule 1 supplies the material factual assurances used by Schedule 2's separate repayment conditions. The assistance fee is earned by performing the agreed homeowner tasks. The truth of the assurances and successful regularisation are relevant to the separate repayment arrangement, rather than to whether already performed assistance was earned.

The draft also preserves the distinction between earned and unearned amounts: a false assurance does not make an unearned fee earned. Schedule 1 creates no additional cash penalty or release of Roque's other obligations. These notes explain the intended structure without determining its legal enforceability; the existing [drafting notes](drafting_notes.md) identify the provisions for legal review.
