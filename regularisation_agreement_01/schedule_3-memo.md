# Schedule 3 — Notes and Drafting Justifications

**For Homeowner review only — not for inclusion in the contractual agreement. This memo must not be attached to, incorporated into, or included in the Agreement or its signing package.**

These notes explain [Schedule 3](schedule_3.md). They add no contractual obligations and are not evidence that Roque, Saege or any individual has committed wrongdoing. The amounts and periods are negotiating proposals, not statutory requirements or established market norms.

## Purpose and timing

The proposed Deposit is funded after complete verification appears satisfactory and the Homeowners accept the basis for proceeding, but before signing or releasing the HDB Acknowledgement form or granting authority. Verification supports the engagement decision; security addresses later-discovered errors and the consequences of unauthorised activity. Satisfactory verification does not eliminate those exposures. No Deposit is needed if the arrangement terminates before funding becomes due.

The five-business-day funding period gives a concrete deadline. Funding does not itself grant authority. Schedule 2's assistance payment remains a separate obligation, including any amount payable before verification. The Deposit is refundable security, not remuneration, a participation payment or punishment for breach.

## Amount and holding arrangement

S$3,000 covers both works together as a provisional reserve for initial independent review and correction. It is not a cost quotation and may be insufficient for substantial testing, reconstruction or litigation. Obtain an independent P.E. quotation covering both installations, records review and corrective submissions before settling the amount; avoid overlapping cost allowances.

A separate jointly held homeowner account avoids assuming a professional escrow service has been appointed or is affordable. It requires cooperation between both Homeowners and leaves Roque exposed to their repayment ability. This is not represented as a professionally administered trust or insolvency-protected escrow. Confirm the bank arrangement permits the agreed joint controls; an independent stakeholder is an alternative requiring its agreement, instructions and fees. Retention and drawdown disputes can reduce the practical value of security.

## Covered events and reimbursement

The Deposit secures a new express reimbursement obligation; holding funds alone would not define when Roque owes compensation. The obligation covers material warranted verification failures, unauthorised continuation or consent use, and improper submissions, with causation, reasonableness and documentation requirements. Honest error can qualify. Minor unrelated mistakes, later changes without an earlier covered failure and unrelated Original Contract claims do not qualify. Ordinary regularisation costs remain Roque's responsibility.

Roque undertakes reimbursement for the specified conduct of Saege and appointees. Saege is not thereby made a party, and no director provides a personal guarantee. If direct recourse against Saege is desired, obtain a separate binding undertaking or make it a party with suitable terms. The Deposit is not a liability cap. A genuine HDB approval does not necessarily cure contractual misconduct or unreliable documents, but approval or termination alone does not prove compensable loss.

Reasonable technical investigation and correction costs are covered expressly. Legal advice needed to investigate or correct the issue is distinguished from litigation costs, which remain subject to law and costs orders. No assumed salary-rate claim for homeowner time is created. Avoid double recovery and obtain legal review of reimbursement scope and enforceability before execution.

## Retention differs from deduction

An evidence-supported claim and reasonable estimate can preserve funds before liability is finally determined. Spending requires an incurred amount agreed or determined under clause 4.3. This avoids treating termination as automatic forfeiture while preserving security during a genuine dispute. It also means disputed security may not provide immediate cash for corrective work; the Homeowners may initially need to fund that work or obtain an agreed interim payment.

Monthly review limits over-retention. The proposed 60-day period after a reasoned dispute requires the Homeowners to advance a disputed claim if they wish to keep the security. It is a negotiated procedural burden, not a statutory filing deadline, and may warrant adjustment to avoid disproportionate proceedings for a small sum. Missing it releases the disputed security rather than extinguishing the underlying claim. Main-agreement dispute resolution applies; do not import the separate termination-challenge or assistance-fee evidential rules.

## Release and closeout

Six months after full completion and records delivery is a proposed discovery window, not a warranty expiry or guarantee that all problems will emerge in that time. Returning security leaves underlying claims intact but removes funded protection for later discoveries. The period is independent of Schedule 2's six-month period after assistance-fee repayment.

Approval alone is insufficient because improper documents might have produced that approval. Objective records and completion conditions establish closeout; homeowner discretion and minor omissions cannot indefinitely prevent it. After termination, the alternative route requires evidence of cessation, notice of ended authority and handling of pending actions. It does not require cancellation of a valid approval or guarantee that HDB will respond to routine correspondence. Specific remaining exposure should support a proportionate reservation rather than a blanket hold.

Refunds are due within 14 days, with accounting. Only qualifying claims notified before release preserve security; their resolution can occur later. No deposit is replenished merely because an issue is discovered after release. The underlying reimbursement obligation survives subject to applicable law.

## Integration required before execution

The main agreement currently incorporates only Schedules 1 and 2. **Schedule 3 is not yet integrated by these new files alone.** Update the main agreement before signing to incorporate Schedule 3, replace references to “both Schedules”, and expressly make cleared Deposit funding an additional condition before acknowledgement signing, authority and implementation assistance. Add survival and security cross-references to its termination and reservation provisions where needed. Align Schedule 1's proceeding restrictions with the funding condition without duplicating the mechanics. This request creates the two new files; it does not amend the existing agreement or schedules.

The existing draft does not automatically terminate for every verification error: warranty timing, materiality and termination triggers differ across Schedule 1. Schedule 3 deliberately preserves those existing rules rather than silently implementing a broader automatic-termination rule. If the intended rule is that every verification error terminates the arrangement, resolve that separately in Schedule 1 and the main agreement before execution. Deposit reservation and reimbursement remain distinct from termination.

Before execution, settle the account particulars, amount, discovery period, disputed-retention procedure, and any alternative stakeholder arrangement. Obtain Singapore legal review of the integrated agreement, particularly reimbursement, holding arrangements and the interaction with termination. Neither the Agreement nor security binds HDB or transfers the Homeowners' regulatory responsibilities to the contractors.
