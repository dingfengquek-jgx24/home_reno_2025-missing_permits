## 1. Material reliance and scope of warranties

1.1 Roque acknowledges that the Homeowners rely on the assurances in this Schedule when deciding whether to:

- enter into the regularisation arrangement with Roque as their contractual counterparty; and
- consent to Roque appointing Saege as the subcontractor undertaking the regularisation of the staircase railings and balcony awning specified in the agreement.

1.2 The assurances are material to the Homeowners' assessment of the honesty, reliability and suitability of both Roque and Saege. That reliance is independent of whether regularisation ultimately succeeds.

1.3 Subject only to specific disclosures recorded under section 5, Roque represents and warrants that each assurance in sections 2–4 is true and accurate in all material respects as at the Effective Date, unless a statement specifies another date or historical period. Roque also warrants that it has not omitted a material fact necessary to prevent those assurances, taken in context, from being misleading.

1.4 Roque must complete the verification and investigation processes described in this Schedule and provide their written results by the Verification Deadline. The **“Verification Date”** is the date of the director's signed confirmation delivered with the complete package; it must be no later than the Verification Deadline. Statements that checks or investigations have been completed refer to the Verification Date, unless expressly stated otherwise; signing alone does not represent that a future check has already occurred. At delivery, Roque warrants that the stated processes actually occurred and that their reported results are accurate. The separate warranties of underlying facts apply as specified in clause 1.3 and are repeated on the Verification Date, subject to any expressly stated historical date or period and only to exceptions accepted under section 5. Performing a check does not excuse an inaccurate factual conclusion that Roque has expressly warranted.

1.5 The Homeowners may rely on the assurances without independently investigating them. Roque must fulfil clauses 1.8–1.10 and deliver the complete written verification package by email to both Homeowners by the Verification Deadline, as the first step before proceeding with regularisation under the main agreement. Existing checks may be used only if they adequately support the assurances at the dates to which they relate. Oral assurances or a promise to provide documents later are insufficient. Missing the delivery deadline is a breach; it does not prevent the Agreement taking effect or suspend Roque's other obligations.

1.6 An inaccuracy or omission is material if it would reasonably affect the Homeowners' willingness to contract with Roque, accept Saege as subcontractor, or rely on the validity or reliability of the proposed regularisation. It need not cause the application to fail. A minor error unrelated to those matters is not material.

1.7 References to Roque's conduct in this Schedule include the conduct of its directors, employees and representatives acting on its behalf in connection with the renovation. This provision does not assume that any particular individual acted for Roque rather than Saege; unresolved roles must be addressed expressly below.

1.8 The director signing for Roque must oversee and review the verification of every factual assurance in sections 2–4, as qualified only by the specific disclosures accepted under section 5. The director may personally carry out checks or obtain assistance from persons with relevant knowledge, competence and access to records, but remains responsible for assessing whether the completed enquiries and evidence adequately support each assurance.

For each assurance, the director must:

- review the relevant records and written reports, including the sources and limitations identified in them;
- require further enquiries or corroboration where a contributor lacks relevant knowledge or involvement, or where information is incomplete, inconsistent or has previously proved inaccurate;
- ensure that lack of knowledge, recollection or involvement is not treated as evidence that an event, decision or conduct did not occur; and
- record unresolved matters and corrections specifically under section 5, without presenting an unresolved matter as verified.

Merely instructing someone to check, assuming checks occurred, or receiving a report without assessing its basis does not satisfy this clause. For assurances concerning decisions, knowledge, disclosure or charging, the enquiries must address the relevant decision-makers and persons directly involved, together with available contemporaneous records. Where an assurance records an unsuccessful enquiry, the verification must support the enquiry and its outcome, rather than imply that the underlying matter was resolved.

## 4. Permit-related decisions, disclosure and charging

Except for the specific facts expressly disclosed under section 5, Roque warrants the following concerning the original renovation:

4.1 Roque did not knowingly decide, direct or agree that the specified works would proceed without an HDB permit required before those works commenced, including by intentionally withholding instructions to Saege for that purpose.

4.2 Roque did not deliberately withhold from the Homeowners its knowledge that a required permit had not been applied for or obtained, or the existence of a decision or arrangement described in clause 4.1.

4.3 Roque did not knowingly represent to the Homeowners that a permit application or approval had been completed when it had not.

4.4 Roque did not charge for or collect payment for permit-related services while having decided that those services would not be performed.

4.5 The omission described in clause 3.1 does not itself establish deliberate conduct, concealment or misleading charging. Clauses 4.1–4.4 are separate assurances about those matters; later establishing a material falsehood in any of them does not require proof that regularisation failed.

## 6. Relationship with the assistance fee

6.1 The assistance fee is earned through performance of the homeowner tasks under Schedule 2 — Homeowner Assistance, Fees and Repayment, independently of whether the warranties in this Schedule are true or breached. This Schedule identifies the material assurances for the second completion condition in section 3 of Schedule 2 governing the separate repayment of earned fees. Sections 4 and 5 of Schedule 2 govern notification, disputes and the agreed treatment of false assurances discovered within six calendar months after repayment.

6.2 Successful regularisation alone does not establish the accuracy of these assurances or satisfy the second condition. A later regulatory action likewise does not, by itself, establish that an assurance was false when made.

6.3 Fees earned for assistance performed are retained unless both completion conditions are met, subject to the agreed later-discovery provision. A warranty breach is not required for those fees to be earned. Fees for assistance not performed are refundable under the unearned-fee provisions regardless of whether a warranty is breached or either completion condition is met. A material falsehood does not convert an unearned fee into an earned fee or justify withholding its refund.

6.4 This Schedule does not itself impose an additional cash penalty, change the assistance fee, or release Roque from its other contractual obligations.
